What a degree actually costs — fees plus living

Tuition is the number colleges publish. Hostel, mess, rent, food and travel are the numbers that decide whether a family can afford the seat. This works out both, per year and across the full course.

Coverage, stated plainly — read this first.

India has roughly 50,000+ colleges. There is no public database that publishes current, verified fees for all of them, and fees are re-fixed every admission cycle by state fee regulatory committees. No tool — mine or anyone's — can give you verified fees for all 50,000. Anything that claims to is guessing at scale.

So this covers two things properly instead of 50,000 things badly. One: named institutions where a real published figure exists — all 23 IITs, all 31 NITs, the IIITs, AIIMS, and the fee bands set by regulation. Two: the living-cost engine below, which applies to any college in India, because it is driven by city and accommodation type rather than by institution. That second half is what makes this usable for a college I have no fee row for — put the college's own quoted tuition into the override box and the rest still computes.

Every figure carries its source. Where sources disagree, I show the disagreement rather than picking the convenient one.

Sources disagree on the IIT total, and the gap is large. One set of sources puts a 4-year B.Tech at roughly ₹8.65–13.9 lakh including hostel. Another puts it at ₹16–20 lakh, on the basis that tuition runs ₹2–2.5 lakh a year and hostel plus mess another ₹1.5–2.5 lakh a year. The likely explanation is what each one counts: mess advances of ₹20,000–25,000 per semester are billed separately and are frequently excluded from the headline total. Living costs by city

This is the half that applies to every college in India, listed or not. Rent is the largest single line — it takes 30–50% of a monthly budget, and a 1BHK in Mumbai costs roughly six times the same flat in Gwalior or Prayagraj.

Monthly transport pass

Metro monthly passes across Indian cities. Single journeys run ₹15–60. Pune's PMC buses are ₹400–700 a month. Transport is the smallest line in almost every student budget — 5–10%.

documented
40%

Private room premium

A private PG room costs 40–60% more than double-sharing. This is the single easiest lever a family has on the monthly number, and it is usually decided emotionally rather than financially.

lever
Rule of thumb worth keeping at the desk. Rent takes 30–50% of the monthly budget, food 15–25%, transport 5–10%, utilities 5–8%. If a family's estimate does not roughly fall into that shape, something has been left out — and it is almost always food or the deposit.
04

Waivers and scholarships that change the number

These are not marginal. At an IIT or NIT they can take tuition to zero, which is the difference between an unaffordable seat and an affordable one. Families frequently do not know they exist.

SC / ST / PwD — full tuition waiver

At all 23 IITs, SC, ST and PwD students receive a 100% tuition waiver regardless of family income. The 4-year outgo drops to roughly ₹1.5–3 lakh, covering only hostel, mess and basic institute fees. NITs operate full or partial remission on the same principle.

tuition → zero

Income below ₹1 lakh — tuition zero

At IIT Kharagpur, General / EWS / OBC-NCL students with family income under ₹1 lakh pay zero tuition. Between ₹1 lakh and ₹5 lakh, tuition drops to ₹33,335 per semester instead of ₹1,00,000 — a two-thirds reduction.

two-thirds off

Merit-cum-Means scholarship

Full or partial tuition waiver for family income below ₹5 lakh. Up to 25% of the batch is eligible — this is a large fraction, not a token scheme.

up to 25% of batch

Institute Merit Scholarship

Tuition waiver for the top 7% of each batch on academic performance, with no income test at all.

merit only

The certificate that fails families

Income certificates for a tuition waiver must come from a Tehsildar or DM-level authority. Sarpanch-signed or self-declared certificates are rejected. The certificate must also be produced fresh every year, with an affidavit on non-judicial stamped paper.

common rejection

Education loans

SBI's Scholar Loan scheme offers up to ₹40 lakh for IIT students at preferential rates around 8.5–9%. Other banks run comparable schemes. Premier-institute admission makes loan approval materially easier — worth saying early to a family that is hesitating on cost.

05

The costs that surprise families

Every one of these is a real line item that is routinely left out of the number a family plans around. The first-semester bill is the shock point.

The first semester is bigger than the rest

It carries one-time admission charges, refundable caution deposits, registration and other initial fees on top of the normal semester fee. Families budgeting on "fee ÷ 8" get caught here.

Mess advance, billed separately

₹20,000–25,000 per semester at IITs, charged on actual consumption and usually excluded from the headline hostel figure. Over 4 years that is ₹1.6–2 lakh that was never in the plan.

Seat acceptance fee

Paid on the JoSAA portal at allotment, then adjusted against the first-semester fee. Largely refundable if you exit before the final round, minus a processing deduction published in the JoSAA bulletin.

Missing the JoSAA acceptance deadline

Described as the single most costly mistake in counselling. One missed deadline is one forfeited seat — no appeal, no second chance within that round. This is a desk process problem, not a student problem.

seat loss

Refundable ≠ free

Caution and security deposits come back, but only at the end of the course. They still have to be found on day one. Delhi's deemed medical colleges carry a first-year one-time admission and security component of about ₹4,47,500.

Air purifier, Delhi

A genuinely odd line that turns out to be real: Delhi's winter air quality between November and February makes an air purifier a ₹8,000–15,000 one-off worth budgeting, plus masks. Small money, but it is the kind of specific that builds trust with an out-of-state family.

Distrust these specifically. Any single figure quoted as if it were exact — the IIT disagreement above shows how far two credible-looking sources can diverge. Any private or deemed college fee, which is re-fixed annually by state committees. Any figure for a college not named on this page. And any living cost more than a year old, since rent moves faster than tuition.
What to do before a family commits money. Get the college's own current fee notification in writing — the PDF, not the listing-site page. For medical, cross-check against the state fee regulatory committee's published schedule. For anything centrally funded, the JoSAA information bulletin is the authority on deposits, deadlines and refund deductions.
